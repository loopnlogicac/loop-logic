One enormous mango changes the story

Nana weighs six small practice bags of fruit. Mina says their usual weight looks close to three marks on the scale. A visitor replaces the last bag with an enormous one. Tuktuk announces that the typical bag has become much heavier. Mina points to the five untouched bags. They need more than one way to describe a collection.

A centre describes a typical location. Spread describes how far observations wander around it. A collection of nearly equal marks and a collection of widely different marks can share the same centre.

You will compare averages, find the middle value, count repeated values and measure squared gaps. You will also learn why a spreadsheet or pandas summary can report a different standard deviation from a population calculation.

Balance, middle and repetition

The mean is the equal share of a total. The median is the middle value after sorting, or the average of the middle pair. A mode is a value tied for the highest frequency.

Imagine dots balancing on a ruler. The mean is their balance point. The median instead asks where the middle positions lie. The modes ask which readings recur most often. These questions can have different answers without disagreement.

The lab's final-observation slider changes one dot. The chart shows each squared gap from the new balance point. Watch all gaps change when one observation pulls the mean: the other dots remain still, but their reference point moves.

From centre to squared gaps

The six readings total twenty. Their mean is 20/6, about 3.333. The middle pair is 3,3, so median is three. Two and three both occur twice; both are modes. The range is maximum minus minimum, or 6-2=4.

A deviation is a reading minus the mean. For these values, the deviations are -4/3,-4/3,-1/3,-1/3,2/3,8/3. Their sum is zero. Adding signed gaps therefore cannot measure how widely the bags differ.

Square each deviation. Their sum is 34/3, about 11.333. Population variance averages those squared deviations across all six observations: 17/9, about 1.889. Standard deviation is its square root, about 1.374. If readings use kilograms, variance uses square kilograms and standard deviation returns to kilograms.

values = other observations plus last observation
mean = sum(values) / count(values)
sort values to find median
for each value: gap = value - mean
variance = sum(gap * gap) / count(values)
std = sqrt(variance)

Replace the six by twenty-four. The total rises by eighteen, so the mean rises by eighteen divided by six: three. Its new value is about 6.333. The middle pair is unchanged, so the median remains three. This is why an outlier, a reading unusually far from the rest, can pull the mean strongly.

Make a human balance diagram

Use the outlier slider to move the last reading from six to twenty-four. Predict the median first. Then compare the squared-gap bars before and after the change. Set every reading to three and decide what both spread measures should become.

Write six readings on cards. Put the cards in sorted order on a desk and mark the middle pair. Share twenty counters equally among six paper cups to represent the mean. You may need fractional counters in the explanation. Replace the last card and compare how much each centre moves.

Make two five-score sets: 3,3,3,3,3 and 1,2,3,4,5. Their means match. Describe their difference before calculating any squared gaps.

Ask which denominator a library uses

The loop program exposes the population calculation, including the six-observation divisor. It uses unrounded arithmetic until the final display.

NumPy computes the same quantities with ddof=0. The explicit argument records that this is a population description. A different convention should be a deliberate choice.

The pandas example describes all fruit sizes. Its fifty-percent entry is the median. The default std in describe uses n-1, so a separate ddof=0 call shows the population standard deviation for comparison.

Count is also useful: many summaries skip missing values. If columns have missing observations, their counts can differ. Always read counts alongside averages before comparing two features or groups.

Do not ask one centre to describe everything

  • Finding the middle before sorting: sort a copy of the observations, then select the central position or pair.
  • Calling the mean the most frequent value: that describes a mode. A mean may never appear in the original observations.
  • Comparing variance directly with readings: variance has squared units. Use standard deviation when you need the original unit.
  • Deleting every distant observation: inspect whether it is a measurement mistake or a real unusual case before deciding.
  • Mixing n and n-1: name the convention so another reader can reproduce the summary.

Optional: description versus estimation

Rafi writes the mean as μ=(1/n)Σxi, where n is the number of values and the sum runs over all observations i. Population variance is (1/n)Σ(xi-μ)². The notation says exactly what the loop does.

When an independent sample estimates an unknown population variance, the usual unbiased estimator uses n-1 after estimating its mean. For one observation that estimator is undefined; population variance for that single observed value is zero.

Spread gives a scale for surprise, not a universal probability rule. A gap of two means something different when ordinary standard deviation is one than when it is ten. Without a distributional assumption, do not attach a normal-curve probability to every such gap. Heavy tails and outliers can make that shortcut misleading.

Describe location and variation together

  • The mean divides the total equally.
  • The median uses sorted middle positions.
  • Several values may tie as modes.
  • Range uses only the extremes.
  • Variance averages squared deviations.
  • Standard deviation returns to the original units.
  • Outliers and denominator choices deserve explicit attention.
