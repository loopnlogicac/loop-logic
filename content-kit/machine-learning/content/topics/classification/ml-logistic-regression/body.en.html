A score needs a meaning before it becomes a decision

Tuktuk gives a new fruit a score of two. Mina asks whether that means two mangoes or two chances in ten. Rafi explains that a score alone has no such meaning. They need a rule that turns the score into an estimated chance, then a separate rule for choosing a label.

Logistic regression connects those stages. It forms a straight-line score from the features, maps the score into a class probability and uses a threshold when a hard label is needed. Its name includes regression, but the task here is classification.

You will inspect supplied coefficients before learning how training chooses them. This keeps the probability mapping separate from the optimisation problem. The lab lets you move weights, bias and threshold independently so you can see which part of the decision each controls.

A smooth ramp between two categories

Imagine a dimmer rather than an on/off switch. Far on one side, the model gives class one a small chance. Far on the other side, it gives a large chance. Near the middle, the model is uncertain. The hard decision comes afterwards, like choosing when the light is bright enough to count as on.

The sigmoid is this smooth S-shaped mapping. A threshold is the probability cutoff used for a decision. A bias is an added score offset, allowing the dividing line to move without changing feature values.

The lab uses centred classroom scores from minus three through three. A negative feature means below the chosen centre, not a fruit with negative physical size. The labels remain apple zero and mango one.

Follow one card through score, probability and label

Multiply each feature by its weight, then add the bias. With weights one and one, bias zero and a card at (1,1), the score is two. The table shows probability about 0.881. At an inclusive 0.5 threshold the hard label is one.

The sigmoid formula is one divided by one plus exp of the negative score. A score of zero gives one-half because exp(0) is one. Larger positive scores approach probability one; more negative scores approach zero. In floating arithmetic, extreme values may round to a boundary even though the mathematical function stays strictly inside.

The six-card calculation keeps both features visible. Swapping the two feature values leaves the score unchanged here because their weights are equal. Changing only one weight breaks that symmetry. Trace the products separately before adding them so that a later unequal-weight example does not inherit an accidental shortcut.

At threshold 0.95, the card with score two changes label while the two cards with score three remain class one. This illustrates why a threshold can reduce positive decisions without moving every card together. The model still ranks these cards in the same order because their scores and probabilities were kept fixed.

Changing the threshold to 0.95 makes the same card class zero. The score and probability did not change. This is a decision-policy change rather than retraining. A threshold should reflect validation evidence and the consequences of different errors, not a belief that 0.5 is required everywhere.

Move the cutoff without moving the score

Start with the supplied weights and note the class-one count. Raise the threshold to 0.95 and identify which cards change labels. Then restore 0.5 and change only the bias. Explain why changing bias moves scores while changing threshold leaves their probabilities alone.

Write scores on six cards and place them beside the probability table. Use a paper strip as the threshold. Move only the strip, keeping cards fixed, and record the decisions. Ask a partner to name which part of the model changed.

Odds compare an event with its complement

A probability of 0.8 means an estimated eight chances in ten. Its complement is 0.2. Odds compare those two amounts: 0.8 divided by 0.2 is four, or four to one. Odds can exceed one, while a probability cannot. Logistic regression uses a linear score for log-odds, then reverses that mapping through the sigmoid.

Do not read a predicted probability column without its label order. Scikit-learn exposes classes_ to name the columns. In the cancer example, class one is benign, so positive does not universally mean disease or danger. The meaning belongs to the dataset's target definition.

Keep estimates, decisions and training distinct

Calling an estimated chance a guarantee

A model can be confidently wrong under missing features, biased samples or changed conditions. Check held-out performance and calibration before treating probabilities as dependable frequencies.

Thresholding the score at 0.5

A probability threshold of 0.5 corresponds to score zero, not score 0.5. Convert the threshold through log-odds when drawing its input-space boundary.

Assuming matching labels imply matching probabilities

The supplied classroom weights and the fitted library weights give the same six labels, but need not give identical chances. The next lesson compares a matched training objective and coefficients directly.

Using an unstable exponential

Computing exp of a huge positive argument can overflow. The practice helper uses a sign-aware sigmoid. Stable arithmetic preserves the intended mapping without suppressing numerical warnings.

Optional: a linear log-odds surface

Write z=w1*x+w2*y+b and p=1/(1+exp(−z)). Then p/(1−p)=exp(z), so log-odds equal z. For a threshold t, the boundary is z=log(t/(1−t)). With two features and nonzero weights, this is a straight line even though probability changes nonlinearly across it.

Training normally minimises a classification loss with optional regularisation. A linear boundary can underfit curved class patterns; adding useful transformed features changes what linear means in the original space. Choose that complexity using validation rather than the final test.

Score, chance and decision are three stages

  • A linear score becomes a probability through the sigmoid.
  • A threshold turns that probability into a hard decision.
  • Changing a threshold does not retrain fixed score coefficients.
  • Read probability columns using the classifier class order.
  • Probabilities express model estimates rather than guarantees.
