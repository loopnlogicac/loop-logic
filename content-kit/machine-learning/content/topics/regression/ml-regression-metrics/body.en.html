Which mistake matters to the family?

A family asks Mina how accurate Tuktuk's rent guesses are. The robot answers with a large squared-error number. Nobody knows whether that means a few hundred taka or several months of rent. Rafi asks for a score in the same unit as the family's budget.

Regression metrics summarise prediction errors in different ways. MAE gives an average absolute gap. RMSE gives more influence to large gaps while returning to the target unit. R² compares squared error with a particular mean reference.

No single score describes every consequence. Two models can share a typical error and fail on different people or locations. A good evaluation names the unit, compares a baseline and inspects residual patterns.

You will calculate four metrics on five predictions, separate an operational baseline from the R² reference, and learn when a score can be negative or undefined.

MAE is mean absolute error: add gap sizes without their signs, then average. RMSE is root mean squared error: average squared gaps, then take a square root. A residual plot places prediction errors against inputs or predicted values so their arrangement remains visible.

In the table, four guesses miss by one mark and one is exact. A score should reflect those five observations, not only their signed sum. You also want to know whether errors grow near high marks or consistently favour one group.

MSE was the training cost in earlier lessons. It remains useful for evaluation, but its squared unit can be awkward for a budget conversation. MAE and RMSE both speak in original target units.

Compute MAE, MSE, RMSE and R²

The absolute residuals are 1,1,1,1,0. Their sum is 4, so MAE=4/5=0.8 marks. The squared residuals are also 1,1,1,1,0 here. Their sum, SSE, is 4 and MSE=0.8 marks squared. RMSE=sqrt(0.8), approximately 0.894 marks.

MAE and MSE happen to have the same numeric value in this example because every nonzero gap has magnitude one. Their definitions and units are still different. Replace one gap by six and the squared contribution becomes thirty-six.

For R², first find the evaluation target mean. It is 6 for targets 2,4,6,8,10. Squared differences from that mean are 16,4,0,4,16, with total SST=40. R²=1-SSE/SST=1-4/40=0.9.

R²=0.9 says the squared error is ten percent of the evaluation-mean reference error on these rows. It does not mean ninety percent of predictions are exactly correct. It does not show that the features cause ninety percent of the outcome.

A poor predictor with SSE=100 on the same targets gets R²=1-100/40=-1.5. Negative values are possible when predictions are worse than that reference. R² has an upper bound of one for a nonconstant target, but no fixed lower bound.

The reference inside R² uses the evaluation targets mean by definition. An operational baseline cannot read future test answers. Fit that baseline using the training target mean and then predict it unchanged for test rows. Its held-out R² need not be zero because the two means can differ.

Give identical scores different shapes

Write residual cards -2,-1,0,1,2. Arrange them against five increasing predicted values. Then rearrange the same cards into a curved pattern. MAE and MSE stay unchanged because the list of sizes is unchanged. The picture changes, revealing why location and order matter.

Sketch a horizontal zero line and plot the five residuals from the marks table. A useful plot has a clearly labelled residual sign. Positive values mean predictions are too low under our convention. If you switch sign conventions, say so before interpreting the vertical axis.

Look for a curve, a funnel-shaped spread or distinct groups. Curvature can suggest a missing nonlinear trend. A funnel can suggest errors changing with target size. Group differences may point to uneven data coverage. These are clues to investigate rather than automatic diagnoses.

Both scores have limits. A random-row split of invented records may not model next year's students or a new institution. A low average error can also hide worse performance for students with fewer resources. Report the data origin and relevant subgroup evidence when the application affects people.

Check units, ordering and reference values

  • Calling MSE a typical number of marks: MSE has squared units. Use MAE or RMSE for original-unit interpretation.
  • Reading R² as classification accuracy: a numeric prediction is not a correct/incorrect class vote. Explain the squared-error reference.
  • Predicting the test mean as a fitted baseline: that consults answers. Use the training mean for an operational comparison.
  • Sorting targets and predictions separately: the pairing is destroyed. Maintain row identity throughout evaluation.
  • Ignoring residual structure: one average can hide systematic errors. Inspect plots and meaningful groups.

Optional: degenerate targets and risk

If all evaluation targets are equal, SST=0. The ordinary R² ratio is undefined for perfect predictions and has a zero denominator for imperfect ones. sklearn r2_score replaces some nonfinite results with finite values by default. Explain that convention rather than treating it as the original formula.

A single evaluation observation also cannot support the ordinary R² calculation. MAE and MSE can still be computed for one paired observation. A metric's availability is separate from whether the evaluation sample is representative enough for a useful conclusion.

For any fixed residual list, RMSE is at least MAE by the root-mean-square versus arithmetic-mean inequality applied to absolute residuals. Equality holds when all absolute residuals are equal. Comparing the two gives some sense of tail influence, but not a full error distribution.

Squared error targets the conditional mean at the population optimum, while absolute error targets a conditional median. If the real cost of overpredicting differs from underpredicting, neither symmetric metric may match the decision. Choose a loss based on the application and explain it before selecting a model.

Evaluating these metrics takes O(n) time and can use running totals. R² needs target variation as well as residual sums. Reliable reporting may add confidence intervals or repeated development evaluation, while preserving the final test from repeated tuning.

Compare models on the same evaluation rows and target scale. A change in the sample can change a score even when the predictor stays fixed. If rows represent repeated students or buildings, preserve those identities when designing the evaluation split.

Report errors people can interpret

  • MAE averages gap sizes in the target unit.
  • MSE averages squared gaps and uses squared units.
  • RMSE restores target units and emphasises large mistakes.
  • R² measures relative squared error, not exact-hit accuracy.
  • A deployable mean baseline learns from training targets.
  • Residual plots preserve patterns hidden by averages.
