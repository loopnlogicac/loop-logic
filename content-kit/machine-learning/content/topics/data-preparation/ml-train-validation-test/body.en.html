Stratification allocates each class a share of the partitions. It helps a small minority remain visible. It cannot manufacture minority examples, and tiny class counts may make an exact ratio or even presence in every partition impossible.

Allocate whole cards from fractional quotas

Three of ten cards belong in test. Label 0 has seven cards, so its ideal test quota is 7 times 3 divided by 10, or 2.1. Label 1 has three cards, giving quota 0.9.

Take integer parts first: two and zero. One test place remains. The remainder 0.9 exceeds 0.1, so give the extra place to label 1. Test counts become two and one; training counts become five and two.

The first program prints counts from the lab's exact recurrence. It uses whole-row quotas, handles an absent class and keeps every row in exactly one partition.

The NumPy-label version cross-checks the same counting procedure. Neither teaching program claims to reproduce scikit-learn's private random stream.

The shared study example first reserves 20% for the final test. It then reserves one quarter of the remaining 80% for validation. The final shares are 60% training, 20% validation and 20% test.

train_test_split uses random_state=0 for reproducibility and stratify=y for class proportions. Two small trees compete using validation accuracy. The smaller depth wins a tie. Preparation medians are fitted on training rows only, and the final test is evaluated after selection.

You may later refit a frozen configuration on training plus validation before one final test evaluation. In that workflow, refit all preparation steps on the combined development set too. Do not revisit configuration choices after reading the final test result.

Leakage can travel through preparation

  • Choosing a seed because it gives the best test score selects a lucky exam. Fix the splitting rule before model comparison.
  • Learning medians, vocabulary or scaling on all rows leaks held-out information. Fit every learned preparation step inside training.
  • Shuffling future events into past training predicts with knowledge from tomorrow. Keep chronological order for forecasting.
  • Assuming stratification fixes bias ignores missing groups and collection problems. Check what the labels and population represent.

Optional: what a score estimates

A held-out score estimates performance under the data-generating conditions represented by the held-out rows. If future homes, schools or seasons differ, the estimate may not transfer. Splitting design should reflect the intended deployment population.

For time-ordered data, train on earlier observations, validate on later observations, and test on the latest period. Ensure features use only information available at each prediction time. A rolling average computed using future rows leaks information even when the row partitions are chronological.

Exact stratification requires feasible class counts and partition sizes. Scikit-learn may raise an error when the least populated class has too few members. Collect more data, change the evaluation design, or explain the uncertainty; do not silently duplicate minority rows into both parts.

Match the split to the prediction

  • Training fits parameters and preparation statistics.
  • Validation chooses configurations without opening the final exam.
  • Test estimates the chosen system's unseen performance.
  • Stratification approximates source class proportions with whole rows.
  • Seeds reproduce a stated algorithm, not every library's stream.
  • Group and time boundaries matter when deployment changes people or dates.
