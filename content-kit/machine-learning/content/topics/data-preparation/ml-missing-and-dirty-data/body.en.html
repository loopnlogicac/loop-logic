An empty square is not a zero

A splash of rain smudges Nana's delivery notebook. One basket's weight disappears. Tuktuk writes zero and reports a basket with no fruit. Mina holds up the heavy basket. The weight exists; they have lost its record. They need an honest way to mark what they do not know.

Missing measurements and incorrect measurements need different questions. A blank attendance entry does not prove zero attendance. A thirty-hour study day contradicts the meaning of hours per day.

You will find gaps, compare replacement strategies, check duplicates and investigate unusual values. Cleaning decisions must preserve the distinction between an observation and an estimate.

Keep the gap visible before filling it

A missing value is an unknown entry. Imputation fills it with an estimated replacement. An outlier lies far from many other observations; that alone does not prove an error.

Fit first, fill second

The observed x values are 2, 4, 6 and 8. Their sum is 20; dividing by four gives mean 5. Their middle pair is 4 and 6, so their median is also 5. For y, sorted observations are 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6. The median is 3; the mean is 17 divided by five, or 3.4.

collect observed training values for each column
fit its mean, median or most frequent value
copy observed cells without changing them
replace unknown cells with the fitted column value
reuse that fitted value for validation and test rows

Median filling gives x replacements 5 and 5, and y replacement 3. The completed table totals 50. Mean filling changes the y replacement to 3.4. Replace the observed 8 with 40: x mean jumps to 13, while its median stays 5. Median is less sensitive to that extreme observation.

For a category, a mean is meaningless. Most-frequent filling chooses an observed category, with a documented tie rule. An explicit Missing category may be more informative. Neither method guarantees the unknown original value.

Dropping rows can be reasonable when few rows are affected and their loss does not distort representation. It can be harmful when one school or district has more missing records. A column missing almost everywhere may need recollection rather than a confident-looking estimate.

Compare the damage from one large entry

Select the large-observation preset and switch between mean and median. Predict which cells change before stepping through the trace. Write down which numbers were measured and which were supplied by the rule.

Make six measurement cards and cover three cells with paper. Let one friend compute replacements and another record the original covered values. Compare them afterward. A tidy table does not mean the estimates recovered the truth.

For duplicated rows, ask what one row represents. A repeated import of the same transaction should be removed. Two different homes may legitimately share every measured feature. Keep identity and event definitions in mind before calling drop_duplicates.

From paper statistics to dirty CSVs

The explicit program fits medians independently and copies the observed cells. Its default output matches the first lab preset.

SimpleImputer learns replacement values during fit. Its statistics_ attribute holds the fitted column values; transform reuses them. strategy="most_frequent" handles categories, while mean and median are for numeric columns.

The rent outlier is 500,000 BDT. Investigate its source and meaning. Do not use the test target distribution to invent a convenient removal threshold. If a rule excludes impossible feature values, write that rule before inspecting outcomes.

Clean without inventing certainty

  • Replacing every blank with zero invents a measured value. Preserve missing markers until you choose a justified rule.
  • Fitting an imputer on the whole dataset leaks test distribution information. Fit on training rows and reuse it.
  • Deleting all distant points may erase rare but valid cases. Check units, source records and context first.
  • Imputing an unknown target creates invented answers. For supervised training, usually exclude rows without verified targets.

Optional: missingness changes what you can learn

For observed values x, the mean minimises the sum of squared distances to a constant replacement. The median minimises the sum of absolute distances. This explains their different sensitivity to extreme values; it does not establish either as the true missing measurement.

Mean filling shrinks apparent spread because inserted values sit at the centre. Missingness may depend on circumstances absent from the table, so dropping or filling can bias conclusions. A missing-indicator feature can record which entries were estimated, but it also needs validation.

An entirely missing training column has no observed mean or median. Define a fallback or remove it deliberately. Library defaults for empty columns can differ by strategy and version, so inspect the resulting shape instead of assuming every column survived.

Make uncertainty part of the record

  • Count missing entries by column before acting.
  • Fit replacement statistics from observed training values.
  • Keep real observations unchanged during filling.
  • Compare dropping with mean, median or category strategies.
  • Check impossible values separately from unusual values.
  • Explain duplicate removal using the meaning of a row.
