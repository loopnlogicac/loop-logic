Compare rankings before choosing one cutoff

Tuktuk ranks six cards by how strongly they resemble mangoes. Mina tries a strict cutoff and misses some mangoes. Nana lowers it and more apples are flagged too. Rafi draws each trade-off on one chart so they can inspect the ranking across many cutoffs.

A ROC curve follows that sweep. It compares the fraction of actual positives found with the fraction of actual negatives falsely flagged. AUC summarises the area under the curve and has a useful ranking interpretation.

You will build the curve from six known scores, handle equal scores together and separate ranking quality from a final decision policy. A large area does not choose the operating threshold or guarantee reliable probabilities.

True-positive rate, or TPR, is recall. False-positive rate, or FPR, divides false positives by all actual negatives. A ROC curve plots FPR horizontally and TPR vertically while the threshold changes.

The upper-left direction is attractive: many positives found with few negatives flagged. The lower-left point means nobody was called positive. The upper-right point means everybody was. Which intermediate trade is acceptable depends on the application's decision costs.

FPR is not one minus precision. Precision asks about the composition of predicted positives, while FPR asks about errors within actual negatives. Keep those groups separate even when a particular small example gives coincidentally equal numbers.

AUC compares positive-negative pairs

Choose one actual positive and one actual negative. AUC is the chance that the positive has the larger score, with a tied pair receiving half credit. In the six-card example there are nine such pairs. Positive score 0.45 wins two; scores 0.8 and 0.9 win three each. Eight credits out of nine give AUC about 0.889.

The same area comes from trapezoids under the curve. Its horizontal stretches contribute two-ninths, one-third and one-third, totaling eight-ninths. Vertical moves have zero width and add no area. The calculation uses the curve's ordering rather than the distance between probability values.

A strictly increasing transformation of all scores preserves their order and therefore their AUC, even if it changes their calibration. That is why AUC cannot tell you whether a displayed 0.8 really behaves like an eighty-percent chance. Evaluate calibration separately when probabilities drive decisions.

Score direction matters. Supplying a score for the negative class reverses the intended ranking, even if the probability column looks familiar. In the six-card example, reversing every score changes AUC from eight-ninths to one-ninth. Read the classifier class order before choosing a column. A result below one-half can indicate a reversed score or a poor model; investigate the target definition rather than flipping answers after inspecting a final test. Do not ask area to answer every evaluation question

Supplying only hard labels

Hard decisions discard most of the score ranking and produce very few operating points. Use the model's appropriate continuous score for the declared positive class.

Breaking ties favourably

A tied-score group has no internal ranking information. Process it together and give tied pairs half credit rather than letting label-aware ordering improve the curve.

Computing without both classes

TPR needs actual positives and FPR needs actual negatives. Report undefined or unavailable when either group is absent.

Ignoring prevalence and operating costs

High AUC can coexist with many false alarms among rare positives. Inspect precision, class prevalence and the chosen operating point rather than presenting one area as the whole decision report.

Optional: ranking and deployment answer different questions

For binary labels, empirical AUC averages pair credits over all positive-negative pairs. The equivalent trapezoidal ROC calculation groups equal scores and includes the endpoints. The reference code uses a simple exhaustive threshold count, suitable for small teaching sets.

ROC normalises within each actual class, while precision also depends on the class mixture. A future population can have different prevalence or score distributions. Keep the evaluated population, positive-label definition and score direction explicit when comparing AUC reports.
